Root design document · how the game works

RuinaA sandbox world you carry on your back

Grow it, pack it, and haul it across land, sea and sky to wherever it is worth the most. Build a home with materials you carried there yourself. Fight with a deep classless build and movement that plays like a platform fighter.

Design pillars

What every system has to serve

CARRY

Goods are things, not numbers

A trade pack sits on your back, slows your stride and keeps your glider shut. A cart holds four. A ship holds sixteen. If it can be carried, it can be lost.

SEAMLESS

Land, sea and sky are one map

Glide off a crag, land by the docks, row to an islet. No loading screens between ways of travelling, and every route is a choice.

DISTANCE

Far and risky pays more

Prices grow with distance and danger, fall as a market fills, and reward fresh goods. The best money is always one more leg away.

OWN

A piece of the world is yours

Farms you planted and a house you raised stage by stage. Ownership is earned with your hands, never bought in a store.

MOTION

Moving is the fun part

Sprint, slide and double jump on foot; dive and flare a glider; drift a roadster. Travel should be the thing you look forward to.

FAIR

Pacing, never paywalls

Labor paces gathering and crafting the way it did in ArcheAge, but generously, and nothing that paces play is ever for sale.

World

Emberholm, the starter isle

A green island about a kilometre across at the foot of the world tree, built so that every way of travelling has somewhere to go. The village sits on its harbour; farmland lies east, house plots west, a crag to the north with a ledge path to its top, and two trading posts that can only be reached the long way round.

Drawn by the game's own terrain generator from shared/zones/emberholm.json. North is up. Hover a place for what is there; in the game, north is +Z and east is −X.

How the island is made
  • A zone is a recipe: coastline lobes, bays, sea cliffs, hills, mountains, ledge paths, flattened town ground and roads. The generator turns it into a 2 m height grid and a surface type per cell (grass, road, sand, rock, soil, plaza, forest).
  • The rules and the renderer read the same grid, split into the same triangles, so feet always land on the drawn ground.
  • Buildings, props, decks (piers, the glider launch) and their colliders come from shared/structures.json, so moving a building in data moves it everywhere.
System

Getting around

On foot you are fast and agile. Water slows you, then floats you. From any height a glider turns a fall into a flight, and rising air at the Windspire's foot will carry you back up.

Can you glide there from the Windspire?

Range at cruise, still air. The Windspire's launch deck is at 128 m.

Glider handling
  • Jump, double jump, then jump again in the air to open the wing. Press jump once more to fold it and drop.
  • Push the stick where you want to go and the glider turns toward it, banking. Sprint dives (faster, sinks fast); crouch flares (slower, barely sinks). Speed you shed in a flare comes back as height, so a dive and a flare can clear a ridge.
  • Below stall speed you drop. Touching ground or water folds the wing. A trade pack on your back keeps it shut.
  • F in flight is the wing's Gust: a burst of speed and lift that needs time to recover (Wayfarer every 30 s, Swiftwing every 16 s).
System

Vehicles & mounts

Every vehicle is a deed in your bag. Summon it and walk to its seat to drive, or to its bed to load and unload packs. Boats appear at the nearest water deep enough for their keel.

Wheels

A wheeled vehicle keeps its own velocity and only grips the road sideways, so throwing the handbrake mid-turn breaks traction and slides. Roads and the plaza allow full speed; grass, forest and rock cap it; sand drags. Slopes pull you down them, a crest throws you into the air, and anything steeper than the vehicle can climb is a wall.

Hulls and sails

A boat runs aground in water shallower than its draft. The rowboat goes where you row it. The sloop's throttle is its sail: W raises it, S lowers it, and what the sail gives depends on the angle to the wind, which wanders round the compass. Wind behind you gives everything; wind in your face gives a quarter.

Shipyards

Big ships are built, not bought. A design from Captain Ilsa claims the Lanternwick slipway; then the hull rises a stage at a time as you haul each stage's material packs to the slip and put in the labor, the same way a house goes up. Launching her clears the slip and puts her deed in your bag.

    Trade & pack running

    Local produce becomes a trade pack at a specialty workbench. The pack rides on your back, slowly, or in a cart or a hold, and sells at a trading post somewhere else. Farther routes pay more; every sale lowers that post's appetite for that pack; fresh goods earn a bonus that sours as they sit.

    Freshness

    Multiplier on the sale price by how long ago the pack was made.

            Housing

              System

              Economy & labor

              Two currencies and one resource. Coins buy almost everything. Charter Seals come only from trade routes and buy land and the best gear. Labor is your capacity for honest work: it refills with time and rest and is spent on every harvest, haul and build.

                Onboarding

                The starter questline

                Thirteen quests that each teach one way of living on the island, unlocked by finishing what comes before. Objectives count what you actually do (planting, gliding, delivering, building), so the tutorial is the game.

                System · built before this document

                Combat & skills

                The original prototype: platform-fighter movement, weapon combos, ground-aimed spells and classless skill trees in the spirit of Path of Exile 2, backed by a Rust battle server tested to agree with the browser frame by frame. It runs unchanged in the open world.

                Engineering

                How it is built

                Content is data, rules are deterministic, and the renderer only ever reads. That is what lets the same rules run in the browser now and on a server later.

                ContentEvery number and placement: vehicles, crops, packs, quests, zones, structures.shared/*.json
                RulesFixed 60 Hz steps. Movement and combat in the Sim; trade, farms, houses and vehicles in Life, changed only by queued commands.src/sim/ · src/sim/life/
                PresentationThree.js scene, procedural toon models, DOM HUD and panels. Reads the rules' state; never changes it.src/render/ · src/ui/
                World serverPlanned: the same Life commands as requests, state in a database, other players' carts and ships streamed by interest.server/ (battle server today)
